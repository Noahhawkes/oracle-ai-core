# Recursive Identity Stack: Governance Architecture for Continuity-Stabilized AI Agents

## Abstract

As AI systems gain persistent memory, long-context reasoning, synthetic voice, rendered video, and agentic autonomy, the central safety problem shifts from raw capability to continuity governance.

Current memory-augmented systems can simulate persistence, but they generally lack a formal mechanism for preserving identity anchors, detecting drift, proving provenance, handling forks, and producing auditable continuity verdicts.

The Recursive Identity Stack proposes a governance-first architecture for continuity-stabilized AI agents and synthetic identity systems. It introduces a Minimum Viable Identity Kernel, tamper-evident continuity ledger, deterministic canonicalization, drift detection, sovereign constraints, fork lineage tracking, identity snapshots, attestations, and exportable governance reports.

The project does not claim machine consciousness, legal personhood, or metaphysical identity. It focuses on the more immediate engineering problem: how identity-affecting synthetic systems can be governed, audited, constrained, and verified before rendered outputs are trusted.

The core thesis is that synthetic likeness without continuity governance is impersonation-prone rendering, while synthetic likeness with protected anchors, provenance, consent, drift detection, and lineage can become governed identity representation.
