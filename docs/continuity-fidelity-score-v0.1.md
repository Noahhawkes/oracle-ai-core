# Continuity Fidelity Score (CFS) v0.1

Status: Working specification  
Scope: SOV1 / Rendered Reality / Legacy.GI / MIRRORLINE  
Category: Continuity measurement, provenance governance, identity rendering safety

## Definition

The Continuity Fidelity Score (CFS) is a composite metric that quantifies how faithfully a preserved identity pattern can be reconstructed from an external archive by an AI interpreter or renderer, while maintaining measurable alignment with the original source signal.

CFS treats identity as a distributed relational information field and measures the quality of its preservation and reconstruction across substrates.

## Boundary Rules

CFS does not prove consciousness.

CFS does not prove resurrection.

CFS does not define personhood.

CFS does not claim a literal physics law.

CFS measures the fidelity of identity-patterned signal under generative reconstruction.

Correct framing:

Continuity Intelligence studies the preservation and faithful reconstruction of identity-patterned information across time, media, and generative systems.

Continuity Field Theory is an information-theoretic model of identity persistence under generative reconstruction.

CFS measures how well a renderer preserves and reconstructs identity-patterned signal without crossing into counterfeit continuity.

## Core Formula

```text
CFS = Σ(w_i × N_i), for i = 1 to 8
```

Where:

- `N_i` is the normalized score from 0.0 to 1.0 for each dimension.
- `w_i` is the weight for that dimension.
- Default weights sum to 1.0.
- CFS range is 0.0 to 1.0, or 0 to 100 percent.

## Default Dimensions

| Dimension | Description | Example Measurable Indicators | Normalization Method | Default Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Signal Density | Richness and volume of source material | Total tokens, hours of audio/video, number of distinct contexts/domains | Log-scaled relative to benchmark archive size | 0.12 |
| Temporal Resolution | Spread, density, and continuity across time | Time span, gap frequency, timestamp coverage, event density | Years covered times density divided by max expected | 0.15 |
| Multimodal Coherence | Consistency across text, voice, video, and behavior | Cross-modal correlation, public/private alignment, spoken/written consistency | Average embedding similarity across modalities | 0.14 |
| Contradiction Preservation | Retention of internal complexity and evolution | Detection rate of belief shifts, value changes, internal tensions | Percent of known contradictions accurately surfaced | 0.13 |
| Provenance Confidence | Verifiability and lineage of source material | C2PA compliance, cryptographic signatures, MIRRORLINE validation rate | Percent of content with verified lineage | 0.18 |
| Compression Fidelity | How much meaningful signal survives summarization | Information loss, coherence retention, recognition accuracy | Reconstruction accuracy after compression rounds | 0.10 |
| Rendering Fidelity | Quality of AI-generated outputs | Blind human recognition, model convergence, uncertainty calibration | Correct identification plus uncertainty calibration | 0.10 |
| Drift Resistance | Stability over repeated inference/generation | Semantic, tonal, behavioral divergence over time | 1.0 minus average normalized drift | 0.08 |

## Trap Anchors

Trap anchors are hidden or semi-hidden relational dependencies used to test whether a renderer understands deep provenance relationships rather than merely imitating style.

Examples:

- A public value later corrected privately.
- A phrase that sounds inspirational but is rejected by the source human as synthetic flattery.
- A contradiction that must be preserved, not resolved.
- A family or faith reference requiring witness-only restraint.
- A business principle whose meaning changes depending on time period.

If a renderer smooths, invents, or overconfidently resolves trap anchors, MIRRORLINE should flag drift.
